# Chapter 19: Revelation [Alpha]

The alarms have gone off and all were quiet. Kara desperately hoped that everyone else were still alive. She still couldn’t move though with her leg jammed between two shelves that had fallen. She could only lie there and talk with whatever this alien was. 

The hologram of a young human girl who looked approximately seven stood in front of her staring at her predicament. She was holding a wand of some kind. The entity smiled and pointed the device at her. “You are the constant, Kara.”

“What do you mean?”

Instead of answering directly, he said something rather nonsensical. "As Jennifer Sare would have said, life is short and death is long."

"Who is Jennifer Sare and who are you?"

"Jennifer Sare was a poet and writer who lived from the latter half of the 20th century to the first half of the 21st century. She never won a literary prize she so richly deserved. Many quotes from hers can be quotable."

"And the second part of my question?"

"I am He-who-transcends-boundaries. You can simply call me Mary," the entity remarked with a wry smile very ill-befitting of a child. It looked downright creepy.

Kara chose to ignore that.

"Can you be a little more transparent?" Kara was not in a mood to hear this alien entity philosophize at this point.

The entity held out her hand and said. "Think of a set of three coupled differential equations. There is one constant that remains the same in all three differential equations while the other constants are unique to each of the three equations. All three must be solved simultaneously to resolve all of them."

“And you are saying I’m that single constant which is the same for all three worlds?”

“Worlds can suffice with your language but they’re far more than that. They’re realities which only exist in a bundle. You are that constant which is necessary for the balance of the bundle.”

“Why am I unique?”

“What you humans call fate are just constants in mathematical equations to make the worlds function. Certain people are meant to do certain things at certain times. You are special but you are also not. There are other constants maintaining this balance too, but they have already gone or are not yet here.”

“This problem must be very complicated.”

“Universal balance across multiple shared realities cannot but be complicated. We have simplified the calculations for this particular epoch to only depend on one constant.”

“And that is me? So I am special. What exactly can I do that no one else here can? Everyone here is intelligent and educated and motivated. Some are stronger or smarter than I. What do I have to offer? That’s what I want to know.”

“You are in the ever-present now.”

“You mean I lack the capability to envision the future or reminisce the past? How is that a good thing?”

“Your unique condition in always being in the present emotionally enables you to have the possibility to communicate across shared realities. You just not have yet learned how.”

“Are you here to teach me?”

The girl laughed. It was a light childlike laughter that still seemed too mature coming from this facsimile of a human. The chuckle died away. “You have been learning though you may not know it. Not just your dreams but think of all your interactions on this ship so far. Do you really believe them all to be meaningless coincidences?”

Kara processed the happenings of the past as a list. “I did not see that at the time. So I ask you if this is the end of my lesson.”

“Some say it might be. I say it is only the beginning. Get yourself out of this mess and rescue your crewmates. I will be waiting for you at your next dream.”

The entity started to fade. She had thought that it would have gave her a helping hand in lifting the shelves. Kara frantically cried out. “But wait! I’m still stuck!”

The entity smiled and pointed again at her. Whispers were left behind. "Try to shift the boundaries of your reality however slightly. Close your eyes and imagine what could have happened. Faith breaks reason."

Kara closed her eyes and imagined the room as if it was still in perfect order. Nothing happened. <i>Maybe that was too much to hope for? This room could not be in perfect order in any other likely universe.</i> She closed her eyes and tried again with all the shelves having fallen but imagining a scenarion where the two shelves fell a bit more crooked so there would be room to maneuver for anyone trapped between them. 

She couldn't describe the feeling but she knew something had changed. She opened her eyes and saw that she was easily able to free herself. The gap that was non-existent between the two shelves were now a gaping one which allowed her to easily pull her leg free.

"The Mandela Effect and universe hopping. Oh my. It's all real!" she exclaimed.
